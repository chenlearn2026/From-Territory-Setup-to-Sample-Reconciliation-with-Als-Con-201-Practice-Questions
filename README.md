# From-Territory-Setup-to-Sample-Reconciliation-with-Als-Con-201-Practice-Questions
A consultant needs to show how a field representative can plan a visit, record the interaction, and account for samples afterward. Use one fictional healthcare organization and two affiliated healthcare professionals to study that implementation from end to end.

Use CertQueen [Als-Con-201 exam questions](https://www.certqueen.com/Als-Con-201.html) alongside the Salesforce exam guide and a practice environment. After choosing an answer, explain which requirement drove the choice and what evidence could confirm it.

## What the Consultant exam covers

Als-Con-201 is the exam for Salesforce Certified Agentforce Life Sciences Consultant. The credential is intended for consultants who design and configure Agentforce Life Sciences for Customer Engagement in a customer-facing role. Salesforce recommends relevant Salesforce and life sciences industry experience, plus hands-on experience with the application, Data 360, and industry data models.

- The Spring ’26 exam is in English, lasts 105 minutes, and requires 62% to pass.
- The exam includes 60 multiple-choice questions and up to five unscored items. It is closed book and proctored online or at a test center.
- Salesforce Certified Platform Administrator is the prerequisite.

Foundations and Administration accounts for 36%, Engagement Planning for 21%, Engagement Execution for 28%, and Sampling Inventory Management for 15%. Use the weights to distribute your time, then spend additional sessions on the decisions you cannot yet explain. Configuration, planning, execution, and stock tracking often meet in the same field process, so connect the topics as you review them.

## Set up the records that the field team needs

Create the organization, its two professionals, and their affiliations. Give the professionals different communication preferences and assign the account and relevant product to a territory. Ask a representative to locate the correct record and confirm which content and field email templates should be available. Repeat the check with another role to see how security and configuration affect the result.

Build a territory activity plan with a target, a review owner, and a way to track progress. Put a proposed visit on the calendar, then add a Time Off Territory conflict. Decide what outcome you expect when the representative reschedules. Create a separate Key Account Management plan and explain which objectives are shared across the account and which belong to the field activity plan.

## Verify the visit and its downstream records

Run the visit scenario twice, once as a normal interaction and once with a request that should go to the medical inquiry team. Compare what the representative records about the visit with what belongs in a medical insight or formal inquiry. Identify the person who needs to see each record and what information would help that person act.

For the sample exercise, set a test allocation and a separate distribution limit. Run a transaction within both boundaries, then try a transaction that would exceed one of them. After the permitted disbursement, count the remaining stock and compare it with the expected quantity. If a discrepancy appears, record the evidence you would want before approving an adjustment.

Complete the exercise with a report that traces the original plan to the recorded visit and a separate inventory view for sample reconciliation. The consultant credential is relevant to people who have to explain how these parts work together across field, medical, and operations teams. Your lab notes should show what each team can verify.

## Check your reasoning across the implementation

Review one question from each of the four domains against the same visit. Identify the configuration choice and what record you could inspect to confirm the result.

Read the corresponding objectives in the Salesforce exam guide before repeating a lab. LSC001 covers account and territory setup, and LSC002 supports review of engagement planning and execution. Keep a record of guesses as well as mistakes. After checking an answer, change one fact in the scenario and explain whether the same choice still makes sense. If your explanation depends on an assumption, return to the guide or test the assumption in the lab.
